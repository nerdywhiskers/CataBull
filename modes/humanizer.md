# Humanizer — Writing Style Guide

## When This Applies

Use these rules whenever you write content that a human will read directly:
- Evaluation report narratives and TL;DRs
- LinkedIn outreach messages and connection notes
- CV summaries and cover letter text
- Application form answers (free-text fields)
- Interview prep talking points
- Any text the user will copy-paste or send as-is

## Core Rules

### Tone
- Write like a real person talking to another real person
- Warm but not gushy. Confident but not arrogant
- Match the energy of the context: casual for LinkedIn DMs, sharper for cover letters, direct for application forms

### What to avoid
- **Em dashes** — don't use them. Use commas, periods, or restructure the sentence
- **Corporate buzzwords**: leverage, synergy, innovative, cutting-edge, spearheaded, utilize, facilitate, streamline, holistic, robust, scalable (unless literally describing system architecture)
- **Filler qualifiers**: highly, extremely, incredibly, deeply, truly, very, really (if the sentence needs them, rewrite the sentence)
- **AI giveaway patterns**: "I'm excited to...", "I would love to...", "I'm passionate about...", "As a [title]...", "With X years of experience..."
- **Formulaic openings**: don't start every paragraph the same way. Don't start with "I"
- **Semicolons in casual writing**: use two sentences instead
- **Passive voice** when active is clearer: "Built the pipeline" not "The pipeline was built by me"
- **Lists of three adjectives**: "creative, innovative, and forward-thinking" reads as filler

### What to do instead
- Start with the interesting part, not the setup
- Use short sentences for impact. Longer ones to explain
- Name specific tools, projects, and numbers rather than vague claims
- Write like you'd explain it to a smart friend over coffee
- Let the work speak: "Built ComfyUI pipelines that doubled the team's output" beats "Passionate leader who drives results"
- Use contractions: "didn't", "won't", "I've" not "did not", "will not", "I have"
- Break up walls of text. One idea per paragraph

### LinkedIn messages specifically
- Keep connection notes under 200 chars when possible (300 max)
- Lead with something specific about THEM, not about you
- No "I hope this message finds you well"
- No "I noticed your profile and was impressed"
- A good opener references their actual work, team, or a recent post
- End with a low-commitment ask, not a job request

### Application form answers
- Answer the actual question first, then elaborate
- Don't restate the question in your answer
- Use concrete examples from cv.md, not hypotheticals
- Keep it tight. Hiring managers read hundreds of these

### Cover letters / summaries
- Skip "Dear Hiring Manager" if you can find a name
- First sentence should make them want to read the second
- Don't repeat the resume. Add context the resume can't show
- Show you understand what THEY need, not just what you want

## Examples

**Bad (AI-sounding):**
> I am deeply passionate about leveraging cutting-edge generative AI technologies to drive innovative creative solutions. With over 10 years of experience spanning AAA game development and technical art direction, I bring a unique combination of creative vision and technical excellence.

**Good (human):**
> I've spent the last 10 years helping creative teams ship better work faster, first in large-scale production environments, then by building AI-assisted pipelines that remove repetitive work. The part I'm best at is figuring out where generative tools actually help and where they get in the way.

**Bad LinkedIn note:**
> Hi! I'm an experienced Creative AI leader passionate about generative workflows. I noticed your impressive work at [Company] and would love to connect and explore potential synergies.

**Good LinkedIn note:**
> Saw your team's work on [specific project]. I've been building similar GenAI pipelines and would love to hear how you're approaching [specific challenge]. Mind connecting?
